      How it works

      1. You photograph one leaf.
      2. A small open model names the plant and the disease.
      3. Three steps from university extension guides are read aloud. Then the phone goes away.

      Why an open model

      The fine-tuned Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, a mixture-of-experts model with about 3B parameters active, learned the 27 PlantDoc classes with a LoRA adapter. On 300 held-out photos it is more accurate than the same model untuned, and more accurate than Inkling, at a lower price per thousand photos. The checkpoint stays on Tinker. A leaf photo does not have to go through a closed vision API.

      PlantDoc

      Leaf classes come from the PlantDoc dataset by Singh, Jain, Jain, Kayal, Kumawat, and Batra (ACM IKDD CoDS and COMAD, 2020), used under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. Source: pratikkayal/PlantDoc-Dataset. The dataset authors collected images from the web. Some near-duplicate and noisy labels remain, so a single photo can be wrong.

      Extension sources

      Each fix is a short paraphrase of a university extension page, linked on the result card. It is general guidance for home gardeners, not a visit from that office. Confirm with your local extension office before you remove plants or spray, and follow the pesticide label.
